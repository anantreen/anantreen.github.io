About ↑ top

Software System Development is a graduate-level course that equips students with a comprehensive understanding of the principles, tools, and practices involved in designing and building modern software systems. Emphasizing a systems-oriented perspective, the course explores foundational and contemporary topics including Operating Systems, Computer Networks, Software Engineering methodologies, and Database Systems (both relational and NoSQL).

Students will gain practical experience with industry-relevant tools and technologies such as Bash scripting, SQL, NoSQL databases, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, front-end frameworks, RESTful APIs, Python, and Flask. The course focuses on building scalable, maintainable, and secure software systems while fostering an appreciation for system integration, version control, development workflows, and deployment strategies.

Designed for students with a fundamental background in programming, this course bridges theory and practice, preparing them to contribute effectively to complex software development projects in both research and industry settings.

Course Policy ↑ top

  • Teaching and Learning Methodology: This course is designed to help learners understand the principles behind good software system development. Learners will work on real-life problems to promote deeper engagement and understanding while resembling true career situations.
  • Academic Honesty: If A helps B in a Task X, B does not get the opportunity to do Task X, does not learn the associated skill, and becomes dependent and unfit for jobs requiring that skill. Helping should help someone learn.
  • Zero Tolerance on Plagiarism: This course requires students to submit assignments, participate in in-class activities, give mid and final exams, and work with groups on projects. Any form of cheating or plagiarism will result in a score of “0” for projects, assignments, or exams. Egregious cases will result in a grade of “F” for the course.
  • Absentee Policy: Students are expected to attend and participate in all lectures and lab sessions (graded or ungraded). If a quiz/activity is held during a lecture session that a student misses, the student will receive a “0” for it. There will be no makeup classes, quizzes, or activities.
  • Miscellaneous Instructions: Given that the subject is based on building software systems, ensure you have a laptop with a decent configuration. Participate in discussions, interact with the instructor during class and the TAs during labs, and attend class with your personal machine.
  • Use of AI Tools: Students are free to use AI tools such as ChatGPT and Copilot for course assignments/activities. Mention what tools were used and how they were used in the submission report. AI tools can mislead or provide wrong solutions, so the concepts must be understood before using them. Use tools wisely.
  • Submission Deadlines: Considering the course has more than 100 enrolled students, there will be no extensions for graded tasks. It is the student's responsibility to submit assignments or lab activities by the prescribed deadline. Please do not wait until the last minute.
  • Grading Policy: Once a grade for a deliverable is released, students may request a re-evaluation with appropriate reasons up to one week after the grade is posted. After that, prior grades may not be discussed with the instructor. There will be no makeup activity/lab unless prior permission is granted from the Academic Office. Late submissions will not be entertained unless there is a genuine health reason supported by appropriate documentation.
  • Grading Distribution: The course instructor reserves the right to make minor modifications to the distribution based on course progress. The overall distribution is:
